# Prototype

**You own the decision, not the code.** The prototype is a throwaway instrument; the real build follows `feature`.

1. Write the one question the prototype answers, the observation that settles it, and who owns the verdict. Prototype only when an experiment is cheaper or clearer than deciding from existing evidence.
2. Build the smallest experiment in the project's native runtime, near its subject and marked as a prototype, with ephemeral state unless persistence is the question. Alternatives sit behind one labeled switch.
3. Observe the thing being decided (output, timing, a screenshot of each variant); a suitability verdict belongs to its owner.
4. Record the question, evidence, verdict, and decision in `design.md`, or in the reply. Delete the prototype afterward unless the user or project keeps it.

**Reply:** the variants, the evidence, the trade-offs, a recommendation, and the scratch path, stated as throwaway.
